Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 28, 2026 · Last revised for version 1.0.22

FilCam — published on Google Play as FilCam: Pro Manual RAW Camera, package app.filmode.filcam — is a manual camera app for Android published by FightTech. This policy describes exactly what the app accesses on your device and what leaves it. In short: your photos are captured, processed and stored on your phone, they are never uploaded, and there is no account. The app uses the internet for two things only: downloading film looks from the Presets Store when you open it, and sending anonymous usage and crash reports, so that a camera that fails on your phone can be fixed for your phone. The sections below list exactly what each involves, and one switch in Settings turns the reports off. There are no ads and we do not sell any data.

What the app does

The app operates your phone's camera, applies the settings and film look you choose, and writes the resulting image file to your own storage. Everything — exposure, colour, LUTs, RAW conversion and the built-in photo editor — runs on your device's own CPU and GPU. There is no cloud processing of your images, no user account and no sign-in. Nothing the app sends over the internet contains your photos or anything taken from them: its only network traffic is the Presets Store downloads and the anonymous usage and crash reporting, both described below. It also talks to Google Play on your own phone, to show FilCam Pro prices, run a purchase you start, and check which purchases you own.

Permissions the app declares

These are the only permissions in the app's manifest. Android asks you for the runtime ones (camera, microphone, location) at the moment each is first needed, and you can revoke any of those later in Settings → Apps → FilCam → Permissions; the network and billing permissions are granted at install time.

  • Camera (CAMERA) — required. It is what runs the viewfinder and takes the photo. Without it the app cannot function. The camera feed is rendered on screen and written to the file you capture; it is never streamed anywhere.
  • Location, optional (ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION) — used for one purpose only: writing GPS coordinates into the EXIF metadata of the photos you take, on the device. It is requested only if you switch on Save location in photos in the app's settings, which is off by default. The coordinates are stored inside your own image files and are not transmitted to us or to any third party. Leave the setting off and the app never asks for the permission and never reads your location. GPS hardware is declared as optional, so the app installs on phones without it.
  • Microphone, optional (RECORD_AUDIO) — used for one purpose only: recording sound into the videos you shoot with the Pro camera. It is requested the first time a clip records with Record sound on; turn that setting off, or deny the permission, and clips are recorded silent. The sound is written into your own video file on the device and is never transmitted to us or to anyone else. The microphone is declared as optional hardware.
  • Storage, on older Android only (WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, declared with maxSdkVersion="28") — needed on Android 9 and earlier to save your photos into the shared Pictures library. On Android 10 and newer the app writes through the system MediaStore and this permission is neither declared nor requested. The app does not ask for broad access to read your existing photo library.
  • Network (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, WAKE_LOCK) — these are not permissions Android asks you about; they are granted at install time. They are used for two things: downloading the Presets Store's catalogue and looks when you open the store, and delivering a usage or crash report (the wake lock lets a report that is already queued finish sending if the screen turns off). The reporting libraries add one Play Store permission, com.google.android.finsky.permission.BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE, which lets Google Play tell the app which listing the install came from. Nothing else in the app uses the network: there is no ad request, no remote configuration and no upload of anything you shot.
  • Google Play billing (com.android.vending.BILLING) — added by Google's Play Billing library for the optional FilCam Pro purchase. It lets the app hand a purchase to the Google Play app on your phone.

The app declares no contacts, phone, calendar, SMS or background-location permission, and no "all files access". It also declares no advertising permission: AD_ID and the Android Ad Services permissions are explicitly removed from the build, so the app cannot read your advertising identifier.

Your photos and where they go

Photos are written into a folder inside your device's public Pictures directory — Pictures/Filmode by default, or whichever folder name you set — and registered with your media library so Gallery and other apps can see them. You choose the file format (JPEG, HEIF or PNG, plus RAW DNG on devices that support it), the quality and the filename pattern. Photos you take belong to you and stay on your device until you move or delete them; uninstalling the app does not delete them.

The image files carry standard EXIF metadata written by the app — exposure time, ISO, aperture, focal length, white balance, orientation, the date and time from your device, and GPS coordinates if and only if you enabled geotagging. That metadata travels inside the file, so bear it in mind before sharing a photo publicly. It is written locally; we never see it.

The Presets Store

When you open the Presets Store, the app downloads the store's catalogue (filmode.app/store/manifest.json and filmode.app/store/trending.json) and the before/after sample pictures, and, when you pick a look, that look's colour file. They come from our content server at filmode.app, which is delivered through Cloudflare's network. These are plain file downloads: like any web request they reveal your IP address and a standard app user-agent to that server, and they carry no account, no device identifier and nothing from your photos. Looks you download are kept on your phone. If you never open the store, the app never contacts it.

Match my photos picks looks that suit your own pictures — the last few stills FilCam took, or photos you choose in Android's photo picker. The matching and every preview are computed on your phone; no photo, thumbnail or colour data from a photo is uploaded.

Analytics and crash reporting

From version 1.0.1 FilCam includes two Google libraries: Firebase Analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase) and Firebase Crashlytics. They exist for one reason. Manual camera control on Android depends on what each manufacturer exposes through the Camera2 API, and it differs from phone to phone; without a crash report we cannot know that RAW capture fails on some particular model, and cannot fix it.

What is sent.

  • Which features you used, as plain counters with no content — that a photo was captured, saved or shared, that a LUT or a preset was applied or imported, that a recipe was saved, that a long exposure was taken, and which of the two screens (camera, photo editor) you were on; in the Presets Store, that it was opened, which look or pack was viewed or used, and how many looks were matched to your photos; and on the FilCam Pro screen, that it was opened, which plan was selected, whether a purchase started, was cancelled or failed, and whether Pro is active on the phone. Never the photo, never the file, never the name of the LUT file you imported.
  • Crash and error reports — the stack trace when the app crashes or when a camera operation fails (opening the camera, taking a picture, writing a DNG), the name of the operation that failed, and the recent in-app steps that led up to it. Reports are attributed to the app with an app key so we can tell FilCam's reports from our other apps'.
  • Device and app context collected automatically by those libraries: your phone's model and manufacturer, the Android version, the app version, the language and country setting, the screen size, and a randomly generated installation identifier that Google's SDK creates. That identifier is not your advertising ID — the app cannot read your advertising ID at all — and it is reset if you clear the app's data or reinstall.

What is never sent: your photographs or any part of one, thumbnails, file names, folder names, your GPS location or geotags, the location or contents of your imported LUT files, the contents of your media library, your advertising identifier, your name, your email or any account identifier. There is no account, so there is nothing to attach a report to a person by.

The switch. Open Settings in the app and turn off Send usage and crash reports. It is on when you install the app; switching it off stops collection in both libraries immediately, for as long as it stays off, and no further report leaves the device. Uninstalling the app has the same effect.

Who processes it. Google, as our processor, under the Firebase privacy documentation and Google's Privacy Policy. The data lands in our Firebase project and is visible only to us, in aggregate; we do not attempt to identify anyone from it, and we do not sell it, share it or use it for advertising. Retention follows Firebase's own settings: user-level analytics data is kept for 2 months, Google's default, which we have not extended, and Crashlytics reports are kept for 90 days.

No advertising. The app contains no ads and no ad SDK. The AD_ID permission is not declared, so the app cannot read your advertising identifier, and no data from the app is used for advertising or ad targeting.

Data we collect

We — FightTech — operate no server of our own for this app and hold no database of its users. What reaches us is only the anonymous usage and crash reporting described above, through Google's Firebase, and only while you leave that switch on. We never see your photos, your files or your location. There is no account system and no newsletter.

Purchases. FilCam Pro (HDR bracketing, handheld night, long exposure, focus stacking, interval and light-trail modes, and the Presets Store's trending looks and Signature pack) is sold as a monthly or yearly subscription or a one-time lifetime unlock through Google Play Billing. Google processes the payment under the Google Privacy Policy; your card details and billing address are never given to us or to the app. As the seller, Google Play Console shows us the order record for each purchase — order ID, product, date, price and country — which we use only for refunds, tax and support, and not to identify or contact you. The app checks the purchase on your phone with Google's signature and remembers the result locally; it sends nothing about it to us. RAW, every manual control, the built-in looks and the free store packs need no purchase.

Data shared with third parties

No data is sold, shared for advertising or used for ad targeting. Google's Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics receive the usage counts and crash reports as our processor for the reporting described above. Cloudflare delivers the Presets Store's files and so sees the download requests described above. Google Play runs any FilCam Pro purchase itself, as described above. None of them receives your photos.

Data retention and deletion

All app data — your settings, imported LUTs, saved presets and recipes — lives on your device and is removed when you uninstall the app. Photos you have taken are yours and stay in Pictures until you delete them.

Usage reports are kept for 2 months and crash reports for 90 days, then deleted by Google automatically. Nothing else about you is retained anywhere: we run no server and hold no account, profile or database for FilCam.

How to request deletion of your FilCam data

  1. Stop new reports. Open FilCam, go to Settings → Send usage and crash reports and switch it off. From that moment the app sends nothing.
  2. Clear what is on your phone. Uninstalling FilCam — or Settings → Apps → FilCam → Storage → Clear data — removes every setting, preset and LUT the app has stored, and resets its random installation identifier, so any later report cannot be linked to an earlier one. Your photographs are not touched: they stay in Pictures until you delete them yourself.
  3. Ask us to delete reports already sent. Email trunghieu4121993@gmail.com with the subject FilCam data deletion request. Tell us your phone model and roughly when you used the app; we hold no name, email or account that could otherwise point us to you. We reply within 30 days and have the matching Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics records deleted.

What gets deleted: the anonymous usage counters, crash and error reports, the device and app context sent with them, and the app's random installation identifier. What is kept: nothing about you — and, on your own phone, your photographs, which are yours and which we never receive. Anything not deleted on request expires by itself on the schedule above: 2 months for usage data, 90 days for crash reports.
