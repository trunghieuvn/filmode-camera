How it works

## Pick a mode, dial it in, shoot.

1

### Choose your mode

P for program, S for shutter priority, I for ISO priority, M for full manual. The mode is not a menu you dig for — it follows the dials you touch, exactly the way a camera body works.

2

### Dial it in

Drag the ruler for ISO, shutter, EV, Kelvin or focus distance, or tap a value to set it exactly. Watch focus peaking, false colour, the live histogram and the level react before you commit.

3

### Fire the shutter

Tap, press a volume key or start the self-timer. Take one frame or a burst — or, with FilCam Pro, a 3/5/7-frame bracket, a night stack or an intervalometer sequence — then edit the result without leaving the app.

Features

## The controls a camera app usually charges for.

### Full manual control

ISO, shutter speed, exposure compensation, white balance in Kelvin from 2200 to 9500 K, and manual focus distance — each on a ruler you can drag or tap. Lock AE, AWB and AF independently.

### P / S / I / M like a body

Program, shutter priority, ISO priority and full manual — the mode letter in the readout follows what you have set by hand, so you always know which half of the exposure the camera is still deciding.

### RAW DNG, free

Keep the sensor data, not the phone's guess at it. DNG files carry the real capture metadata and no baked-in look, so you can grade them in Lightroom or Darktable. RAW is free — it is not part of FilCam Pro.

### 43 film looks, live

Every look is previewed on your own scene before you press the shutter, then baked into the JPEG. Import your own .cube LUTs, add light leaks, halation, star, dust or VHS from eleven live effects, and warm or cool the frame on the colour pad.

### Peaking, false colour & scopes

Focus peaking, zebra and false colour mark the viewfinder, never the file. A live RGB histogram, parade, waveform and vectorscope, an electronic level, crop guides and ten composition grids — thirds, golden ratio, golden spiral, triangles, diagonals, centre cross, 4×4, 6×6, horizon and dynamic symmetry.

### Focus and metering you choose

AF-C, AF-S, macro, infinity or fully manual focus by distance. Matrix, centre-weighted or spot metering. Pinch to zoom, and map the volume keys to the shutter, exposure or zoom.

### Bracketing, night & interval PRO

Burst and the self-timer are free. FilCam Pro adds exposure bracketing of 3, 5 or 7 frames with your own EV step for HDR, a night mode that stacks frames, focus stacking, and an intervalometer that runs by shot count or by minutes for timelapse.

### Long exposure & light trails PRO

Waterfalls, traffic, star fields and empty night streets. The long-exposure drive runs up to 30 seconds, and light trails hold the frame open from 15 seconds to 5 minutes, keeping the brightest part of every frame while you watch it build.

### Your files, and a photo lab

Write into any folder inside Pictures, with a filename pattern built from date, time, counter, ISO and shutter, as JPEG, HEIF or PNG with full EXIF and optional geotagging. Then finish the shot in the built-in editor: curves, HSL, clarity, fade, vignette, undo and compare.

Exposure modes

## Four letters, the same as on a mode dial.

You never pick the mode from a list. Set a value by hand and the camera hands you that control; leave it on auto and the camera keeps it. The letter in the readout just tells you where you stand.

P

Program

ISO and shutter both automatic. Nudge exposure compensation, frame the shot and let the meter do the arithmetic — with every other manual control still available.

S

Shutter priority

You set the shutter speed, the camera finds the ISO. Freeze a moving subject at 1/1000, or slow it down for water and motion blur.

I

ISO priority

You pin the ISO, the camera picks the shutter. Keep noise where you want it — base ISO for clean daylight frames, higher only when you choose.

M

Manual

Both halves are yours, across the whole range your sensor reports. Nothing drifts between frames, which is what makes bracketing, intervals and night work repeatable.

FAQ

## Fair questions.

**Does FilCam shoot RAW DNG?**

Yes, on phones whose camera hardware reports RAW support — most mid-range and flagship Android phones do, and the option is hidden rather than broken on the ones that do not. DNG is free and not part of FilCam Pro. You can save a DNG alongside a JPEG so you keep both the negative and a ready-to-share file, and the DNG is always the untouched sensor data with no film look baked into it.

**How long an exposure can I take?**

In S and M mode the shutter goes as long as your phone's sensor reports, free — the app reads the real limit from the camera rather than promising a number it cannot deliver. For longer, FilCam Pro adds a long-exposure drive of up to 30 seconds and light trails that stay open from 15 seconds to 5 minutes. Brace the phone or use a tripod, and trigger it with the self-timer or a volume key so you do not shake the frame.

**What is free, and what is FilCam Pro?**

Manual controls, RAW DNG, the film looks, the light effects, the viewfinder tools and the photo editor are free, with no watermark. The free version shows a small banner ad on the Settings screen only, never over the viewfinder or while you shoot, and you can watch an optional video ad to try Pro for 30 minutes. FilCam Pro removes the ads and adds the advanced drive modes — exposure bracketing, night mode, focus stacking, the intervalometer, long exposure and light trails. It is sold monthly, yearly with a 7-day free trial, or as a one-time lifetime unlock, and Google Play shows the price in your currency before you pay.

**Do my photos get uploaded anywhere?**

No. Capture, processing, LUTs and editing all run on your phone's own GPU, and no image data ever leaves the device. There is no account and no cloud library. The app does send anonymous usage and crash reports — which controls got used, and a stack trace when something fails — so that a camera that breaks on your phone gets fixed for your phone; never a photo, a file name or a location, and one switch in Settings turns it off. See the [Privacy Policy](https://trunghieuvn.github.io/filmode-camera/privacy) for the full detail.

**Where are my photos saved?**

In a folder inside your device's *Pictures* directory — *Pictures/FilCam* by default, and you can change the folder name in Settings. Files are registered with your media library, so Gallery, Google Photos and any other app see them immediately. The filename pattern is yours too, built from `{date}`, `{time}`, a counter, `{iso}` and `{shutter}`.

**Does it add GPS coordinates to my photos?**

Only if you turn on *Save location in photos* in Settings, which is off until you do. When it is on, the coordinates are written into the photo's own EXIF on the device — nothing is sent to us or to anyone else. Turn it off and no location permission is ever requested.

**Can I use my own film looks?**

Yes. Import `.cube` LUT files and they appear in the strip under the viewfinder next to the 43 built-in looks, previewed live on the scene in front of you, so the look is baked in the moment you press the shutter. The built-in editor can also save a grade back out as a `.cube` file.

**Which Android versions and languages does it support?**

Android 8.0 and newer. Some controls depend on what your camera hardware exposes — the available ISO and shutter range, HEIF encoding and RAW support all come from the device — so the app shows what your phone can actually do instead of a dead menu item. The app is in nine languages: English, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Arabic, Hindi, French and Indonesian, and you can switch in Settings.

**Where do I get it?**

On [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.filmode.filcam), free to download. Check the package name — `app.filmode.filcam` — rather than the app name: a camera app's name is easy to copy, a package name is not.
